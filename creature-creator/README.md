# Critter Arena

A 3D creature creator for Android. Pick a body type, bend the spine, stick on parts piece by piece, and spin the result around. Runs in landscape, with no accounts. The game is bundled and plays offline; the network is used only for ads.

It is an original take on the "build a creature from parts" idea: its own parts, art and interface.

## What you can do

- **Eight body types:** humanoid, quadruped, avian, insectoid, arachnid, cephalopod, serpent, aquatic. Each starts as a finished creature you can strip down or build on.
- **Shape the body:** drag the white dots to bend and stretch the spine, tap a dot to change how thick the body is there, add or remove sections.
- **About 60 parts:** eyes (round, slit, bar-pupil, glowing, beady, compound, stalk), mouths (muzzle, beak, mandibles, round maw, lips, trunk, feelers, nose, whiskers, tusks, beard), ears, horns, antlers, antennae, crests and hair, legs and arms with a choice of feet and hands, tentacles, three kinds of wing, fins, nine tails, and back pieces (spike rows, plates, sails, manes, shells).
- **Easy placing:** tap a part to add it where it belongs for that body type. Tap it on the creature and drag to slide it anywhere on the body. Parts off the centre line are mirrored automatically. Every part has its own sliders (size, length, curl, bend, teeth and so on).
- **Male and female:** one tap switches between the two. Build, limb thickness and the size of horns, crests and manes change, and the "Difference" slider sets how strongly. Any part can be marked "Male only" or "Female only" (antlers, a mane, lashes).
- **Skin:** smooth, fur (real layered fur with length and shagginess), scales, shell or slick; eight patterns; seven colour slots (main, pattern, belly, eyes, horn and claw, hair and feather, wing skin).
- **Random creatures:** the "Random" card rolls a surprise creature (random body type, shape, colours and parts) that you can keep editing.
- **The Colosseum:** the Battle button enters your creature in a gauntlet of fights to the death (six on the first difficulty, up to twenty on the last) against random creatures, each tougher and better armed than the last, ending with the reigning champion. Its stats come from how you built it: size gives health; teeth, claws, horns and clubs give attack; scales, shell and plates give armour; legs and wings give speed; eyes help it land hits. Fights run by themselves while you time Power strike, Guard and any abilities you have picked up.
- **Animated fights:** legs and arms are jointed, so knees and elbows bend as creatures walk, crouch, wind up and strike. Creatures walk in from the gates, wind up and strike in a style that suits their body (bite, claw or weapon swing, tail whip, stinger, horn charge, wing swoop, tentacle lash), recoil, guard, dodge, stagger when stunned, collapse when killed and celebrate a win. Limbs, wings and tails swing from their joints and fur moves with them. Hits throw sparks and dust, ailments show on the body, heavy blows freeze the action for an instant and a kill plays in slow motion.
- **Rewards after every win:** choose one of three random rewards, in any mix of weapon, relic (a passive that stacks) and ability, each with a random rarity and strength. There are 12 weapons, 38 relics and 15 abilities, built around a handful of themes that feed each other: bleed, poison, fire, stuns, critical hits, speed, guarding, thorns and healing. The draft leans toward whatever matches what you already hold and labels the match.
- **The body decides the build:** a creature holds one weapon per hand (each arm or tentacle is a hand, up to eight; with no hands it carries one in its jaws), and held weapons show on the model. Humanoid bodies get three ability slots, others two. Many abilities and relics only appear for the right body: Dive needs wings, Gore needs horns, Tail Sweep a tail, Venom Spit a stinger or mandibles, Stampede four legs, and so on.
- **Winning and dying:** every fight won pays points (15 for the first, rising with each fight), and the title pays 100 per fight in the gauntlet plus up to 500 for health left, all multiplied by the difficulty's pay rate. If the creature dies it is deleted for good, unless you spend a revive. Closing the app between fights keeps your place. Closing it on the death screen does not undo the death, and leaving in the middle of a fight is forgiven once per gauntlet (the fight restarts); a second time counts as a death.
- **A guided start:** a five-step guide walks a new player through shaping, adding parts, colouring and entering a fight, and the first fight explains Power and Guard before it begins. Both can be skipped.
- **Play preview and idle life:** the Play button in the workshop shows your creature standing, walking and attacking. Left alone for a few seconds it also comes to life by itself (breathing, looking about, stretching) until you touch the screen.
- **Progress that survives:** the save is mirrored into the app's own files, which Android backs up to the player's Google account, so points, unlocks and creatures return after a reinstall or on a new phone. A backup code can also be exported and imported by hand.
- **Sharing and duels:** any saved creature can be shared as a short text code; a friend imports it to add the creature to their own collection, or pastes it at the arena gate for a friendly duel (one free fight, nothing at stake).
- **Reminders:** optional notifications when runs have refilled and when the daily gift is ready.
- **Languages:** English, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, French and German. The game starts in the phone's language and can be switched in Settings.
- **Sound and music:** looping music for the menu and workshop, for fights and for final fights; recorded effects for hits, blocks, swipes, footsteps, falls, buttons, cards and coins; synthesised fanfares, crowd and roars. Music and sound can each be switched off at the bottom of the main menu, and everything goes quiet when the app is in the background. All third-party audio is CC0; see `CREDITS.md`.
- **Runs smoothly on different phones:** fighters in the arena are built lighter than in the workshop, the workshop only redraws when something changes, and if frames come slowly the game steps sharpness and fur density down by itself (and back up when there is headroom), remembering the level for next time.
- **Reasons to come back:**
  - *Difficulty levels.* Novice (6 fights), Bronze (8), Silver (10), Gold (12), Platinum (14), Diamond (16), Master (18) and Legend (20). Harder levels are longer, have tougher opponents and pay more. Winning the title on one opens the next.
  - *The Endless Pit.* Opens after your first title. Fights keep coming and keep getting stronger, with a champion every tenth fight; the goal is to beat your own best. It is an exhibition, so a creature that falls there is carried out alive.
  - *Six arenas.* After the first two fights, about half of all fights move from plain sand to the Mire (both fighters slowly lose health), the Frozen Ring (slower attacks, longer stuns), the Forge (both start to burn after 12 seconds), Storm Court (lightning hits one fighter at random), or the Moonlit Ring (more dodges and critical hits). Each has its own sky, ground, light and weather, and the rule applies to both sides alike.
  - *Opponent temperaments.* Opponents no longer all fight the same way: Berserkers use Power constantly and never guard, Turtles guard nearly everything, Finishers save Power for a wounded enemy, and Tricksters open with abilities and strike while you are stunned. The fight's intro says which you face and how to answer it.
  - *Weekly challenge.* Eight fights with the same opponents, arena and special rule for every player, changing each Monday (UTC). It is an exhibition, so a loss costs nothing; the first win each week pays 400 points.
  - *Elite opponents.* Some fights are against an Enraged, Iron-hided, Swift, Giant, Venomous or Thorned opponent, worth half as many points again.
  - *Daily gift.* Points every day, growing over a seven-day streak, with a free revive on the seventh day.
  - *Goals.* Twenty-one one-off targets that pay points when claimed (win 25 fights, win the title with three body types, hold four weapons, and so on).
  - *Stats.* Every run ends with a summary (fights won, damage dealt and taken, biggest hit, critical hits, blocks, points). The Records screen keeps lifetime totals, your hardest title, your best Endless run and a hall of champions.
  - *Rating prompt.* After a title, or a pit run of ten or more wins, the app asks Google Play to show its rating card: at most three times, at least three weeks apart, and never before the third finished run.
- **Runs and the timer:** you hold up to 5 runs. Starting a gauntlet uses one, and one comes back every 30 minutes of real time, with a live countdown on the main menu and at the arena gate. Resuming a gauntlet, or reviving, does not use a run.
- **Rewarded ads (optional, never forced):** watch one for an extra run, to revive a dead creature at half health (once per gauntlet), to double the points from the fight just won, or to double the title prize.
- **The shop (main menu):** points buy locked body types (humanoid, quadruped and avian are free), extra body parts, more weapons, abilities and relics for the reward pool, revives, and permanent boosts (health, attack, recovery, rerolls, and a reward before the first fight).
- **Settings (the gear on the main menu):** music and sound volume, language, graphics quality (automatic, or fixed high, medium or low), text size, a colour-blind friendly palette for health bars, reduced motion (no shake, flashes, slow motion, weather or idle movement), reminders, replaying the guide, the backup code, restoring purchases, and reopening Google's ad-consent choices.
- **Full-screen ads between runs:** besides the optional rewarded ads, an interstitial can show when a run ends. It is held back on purpose: never in a player's first three runs, at most one every four minutes, not within two minutes of a rewarded ad, and never for players who bought the ad-free upgrade.
- **Extras:** undo and redo, auto-spin, save and reopen creatures, and a Photo button that saves a 1920x1080 picture to `Pictures/CreatureWorkshop`.

## Put it on your phone

You need `app-debug.apk`, either the one from the Claude session or one you build (below). Send it to the phone, open it, and allow "Install unknown apps" when Android asks. A Play Protect warning is expected for a personal build. Needs Android 7 or newer.

Every build is signed with the key in `app/signing/debug.keystore`, so a new build installs over the old one and saved creatures stay.

## Ads and publishing on Google Play

The app uses Google AdMob rewarded ads and Google's consent prompt (UMP). As shipped it uses **Google's public test IDs**, so it shows test ads and earns nothing. Before publishing:

1. **AdMob:** create an AdMob account, add the app, create one *rewarded* and one *interstitial* ad unit, and put your three IDs in `app/src/main/res/values/ads.xml`. Add your own phone as a test device in AdMob; tapping real ads yourself can get the account suspended.
2. **Consent:** in AdMob under Privacy & messaging, create the European regulations message. Without it the consent prompt has nothing to show.
3. **App ID:** the package name is `com.personal.creatureworkshop` (`applicationId` in `app/build.gradle.kts`). It cannot be changed after the first upload, so pick the one you want first.
4. **Signing:** Play needs a release bundle signed with your own upload key, which must not be committed. Create one with
   `keytool -genkeypair -v -keystore upload.jks -keyalg RSA -keysize 2048 -validity 10000 -alias upload`
   then put a `keystore.properties` file in this folder (it is git-ignored) containing `storeFile=upload.jks`, `storePassword=...`, `keyAlias=upload`, `keyPassword=...`, and run `./gradlew bundleRelease`. The bundle is at `app/build/outputs/bundle/release/app-release.aab`. Raise `versionCode` for every upload.
5. **Store forms:** you will need a privacy policy URL, the Data safety form (AdMob collects the advertising ID and device data), the "contains ads" declaration, and the content rating questionnaire (creatures fight to the death, in cartoon style). If the audience includes children, Play's Families rules restrict how ads may be served, so choose the target age deliberately.
6. **Testing:** Google has required new personal developer accounts to run a closed test before production access. Check the current rule in the Play Console.

Things to know about how this is built: runs, points and unlocks live on the device only, so they are lost on uninstall and are not protected against tampering, and the run timer trusts the phone's clock. That is normal for a single-player game but worth knowing before adding anything paid.

## Purchases, analytics and the other things that need your accounts

These are built in but stay switched off until you supply the pieces only you can create.

- **Purchases (Google Play Billing).** In the Play Console, under Monetise > Products > In-app products, create four products with exactly these ids: `patron` (bought once: unlimited runs and ad rewards without watching), `points_small` (2,500 points), `points_big` (8,000 points) and `revives_3` (three revives). Set your own prices; the shop's Premium tab shows whatever Play reports. Until the products exist and the app is installed from Play, the tab shows "Soon". Purchases are granted on the device with no server check, which is normal for a small single-player game but can be cheated on a rooted phone.
- **Analytics and crash reports (Firebase).** Create a Firebase project, add an Android app with your package name, download `google-services.json` and put it in `app/`. The build detects the file and switches to the Firebase version of `Telemetry.java`; without the file there is no Firebase code in the app at all. Events sent: tutorial steps, run start, fights won, runs lost, titles, reward picks, unlocks, ads, purchases, shares, duels, and errors in the game page. Remember to declare analytics in the Play Data safety form and the privacy policy.
- **Cloud backup.** Nothing to set up: it uses Android's automatic backup, which needs the player to have backup turned on in their phone settings. It is not instant sync between two phones in use at the same time.
- **Leaderboards** are not included; they need Google Play Games Services set up in the Play Console.

## Build it

1. Install a JDK (17 or newer) and the Android SDK with platform 37. Set `ANDROID_HOME`, or put `sdk.dir=...` in `local.properties`.
2. From this folder run `./gradlew assembleDebug`.
3. The APK is at `app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk`.

The editor itself is a web page in `web/`, bundled into `app/src/main/assets/www/` (the bundle is committed, so step 2 works without Node). After changing anything under `web/`, run `npm install` once and then `npm run build` in `web/` before building the APK. You can also open `app/src/main/assets/www/index.html` in a desktop browser to try it.

## How it is put together

```
web/src/geom.js        the "tube": a smooth skin over a curved path; nearly every piece is one
web/src/parts.js       the parts catalogue; each part is a small function that builds its shape
web/src/templates.js   the eight body types: starting spine, where new parts land, starter parts
web/src/creature.js    turns a creature (plain JSON) into a 3D model; male/female differences
web/src/materials.js   skin patterns and fur (one shader), eye textures
web/src/main.js        scene, touch controls, panels, save/load, photo
web/src/random.js      the random creature generator (also makes every opponent)
web/src/battle.js      fight rules and how a build turns into stats (no graphics)
web/src/items.js       weapons, relics and abilities, the reward draft, and the shop's prices
web/src/anim.js        poses and joints: how a body with no skeleton is made to move
web/src/fx.js          particles and impact rings
web/src/audio.js       sound effects, music and the synthesised sounds
web/src/i18n.js        the translation layer; web/src/lang/*.json are the languages, keys.json the list of strings
web/src/share.js       creature and backup codes
tests/                 the test scripts (see tests/README.md)
web/audio/             the recorded sound effects (packed into the bundle by web/tools/sfx.js)
web/src/arena.js       the colosseum scene, fight animation and its screens
app/                   the Android shell: a full-screen landscape WebView, back button, photo saving
```

Every model and texture is generated in code. The third-party pieces are three.js, the Fredoka typeface, Google's ads, consent and review libraries, and CC0 music and sound effects; all are listed with their licences in `CREDITS.md`. The body-type pictures on the main menu are pre-rendered files in `app/src/main/assets/www/thumbs/`; if you change a starter creature, re-save them from the browser.

## Tested and not tested

The settings screen, the six arenas and their hazards, the weekly challenge and the idle animation have a scripted browser test (`tests/depth.js`), and 6,000 fuzzed fights now run with random arenas and temperaments. Simulated gauntlets put win rates with the new arenas and temperaments within a few points of where they were before. The interstitial ad, the restore-purchases button and the ad-choices button compile but, like the rest of the Android-only code, have not been run on a device.

The editor was exercised in headless Chromium at a phone-sized landscape screen with simulated touches: every part on every body type, spine dragging, tap to select, drag to move, sliders, male and female, patterns, undo, save and photo. The battle mode was run the same way with the clock driven by a script: gauntlets lost (with and without a revive), a gauntlet won, reward picks including a pre-fight reward and an eight-handed creature, shop purchases, and quitting and resuming. Balance was tuned with simulated gauntlets, rewards included. A fresh starter creature wins the Novice title about half the time, Bronze about one time in six and Silver rarely. A creature built for fighting wins Novice almost always, Bronze two times in three and Silver four in ten; with every shop boost it wins Gold about six times in ten, Platinum four in ten, Diamond two in ten and Legend about one time in fifty. Endless runs usually end between the eighth and the twentieth fight. Each reward was also measured on its own across five builds, and all of them add between roughly a third of a fight and one and a third fights to an average run.

The newer Android pieces (the backup file, sharing, reminders, Google Play Billing, the Firebase variant, the review card) all compile, and the Firebase variant was built once with a placeholder config, but none of them has run on a device. The translations were written by machine and checked only by a script (every string present, placeholders and emoji intact); they have not been read by native speakers, and a few rarely seen messages may still appear in English.

A later optimisation pass measured a fight scene at about 630,000 triangles and cut it to under 200,000 (lighter arena models, every second fur layer), and cut the time to the main menu from several seconds to under half a second by shipping the menu pictures and keeping part thumbnails on the device. Those figures are from a desktop test rig with a software renderer; real phone frame rates have not been measured, and none of the audio has been listened to, only checked to decode and play.

Before release the game went through three QA passes: an independent code review (22 findings, the real ones fixed), fuzzing of 6,000 random fights and drafts, a random-action test of the editor, and a scripted regression of saving, resuming, dying, reviving, ads, ranks, goals and the daily gift.

The run timer and every ad-gated feature were tested in the browser with a stand-in for the ad. The AdMob and consent code compiles against the current SDKs and the release bundle builds, but **no real or test ad has been loaded or shown**, because that needs a device. The rating prompt is the same: the call is wired and compiles, but Google only shows the card in an app installed from Play. It has not been run on a physical phone or an emulator, so the Android-only pieces (hiding the system bars, the back button, saving a photo to the gallery, sound) are untested, fights have not been watched at real speed, and speed on an older phone with long fur is unknown.
